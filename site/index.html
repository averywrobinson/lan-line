Linked in seconds. Muscle memory in minutes.

01

Open on both machines

Launch Lan-Line on your Mac and PC (or several of them). They find each other on your local network—pair once with a short code, no IP hunting required. The code is never sent over the network: it only proves the two computers belong together, and every connection then gets its own encryption keys.

02

Drop, paste, or type

Drag in files or whole folders, paste clipboard screenshots, or send URLs and terminal snippets straight into the pinned window.

03

Copy or drag out

Grab it immediately on the other screen with one click or a drag straight into your editor or folder. Identical in both directions.

Public beta

Help test Lan-Line.

Install it on a Mac and a Windows PC on the same network, use it for a few days, and tell us what breaks or feels off.

Updating: from beta.4, update every computer (beta.5 and later encrypt everything and can’t talk to beta.4; the window says “Update Lan-Line on …”). beta.5, beta.6 and beta.7 work together; folders need beta.6 or later on both ends, and pairing more than two computers needs beta.7 on the ones that pair with several. Your pairings carry over.

What to try

  • Pairing with the short code, both ways (Mac shows it, PC types it, and the reverse).
  • Text, files, whole folders and pasted screenshots in both directions, including a few big files and a folder with subfolders. With three or more computers: send to everyone, then to just one.
  • Minimize one window and send to it: it should pop back up without stealing your keyboard.
  • Storage settings: keep-for-days, the storage limit and “Clean up now”.
  • Light and dark mode, pin on top, and reconnecting after sleep or a network change.

Known issues

  • Not code-signed yet: the first open needs a one-time OK (see the FAQ).
  • Encryption hides what you send, not that you’re sending: someone on your network can still see how much moves and when. It can’t protect a computer that’s already compromised.
  • Mac: macOS 13+ (Apple Silicon or Intel). Windows: 10 (1809+) or 11, 64-bit.

How to report

  • Report a bug: both OS versions, the Lan-Line version and the steps.
  • Share feedback: what worked, what felt off, what you’d change.
  • Get an email for each release: on GitHub, Watch → Custom → Releases.
  • Security problems: report privately, not in a public issue.

Never. Every message, pasted screenshot, and file travels directly from one machine to the other over your local network (LAN). There are no cloud relays, no user accounts, and zero telemetry tracking your transfers. Your files and messages never leave your network. Lan-Line only goes online when you click Check for Updates or turn on daily checks. (That check reads one small file from this site and sends no ID.) On the way, everything is encrypted (AES-256-GCM with fresh keys for each connection), and your pairing code or secret is never sent in the clear.

Yes—bridging Mac and Windows is the exact reason Lan-Line exists. It also pairs Mac-to-Mac and PC-to-PC the same way: one computer shows a short code, the other types it.

Shared clipboards silently overwrite whatever you copied ten seconds ago and struggle with binary files or multi-file drops. Lan-Line gives you a visible, scrollable drop tray so every link, image, and file stays intact until you grab it.

No artificial caps. Because files stream directly across your local network rather than through a cloud server, a 25-byte config file, a 4 GB video export and a whole project folder all move at whatever speed your router or Ethernet switch supports. If the other computer doesn’t have room, it says so before anything is sent.
